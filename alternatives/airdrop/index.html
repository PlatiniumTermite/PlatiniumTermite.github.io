Comparison

Flow vs AirDrop — honestly.

AirDrop is the benchmark — but it only exists between Apple devices. Flow recreates the experience for people whose devices aren't all from one company. Here's the truthful scorecard.

Identical in daily use

Discover → tap → approve → send

Same flow: your other device appears automatically, you tap it, the owner approves, files fly with progress.

Files, folders, text, links

Everything AirDrop carries, Flow carries — including whole folders and send-to-many.

Universal Clipboard

Copy on one computer, paste on the other — text and images, both directions, no send step at all.

No window required

Menu bar / tray agent means the device is always there — like AirDrop's always-on presence.

Nearest equivalents — different path, same result

  • No shared Wi-Fi. AirDrop uses Apple's private AWDL radios to work offline — a locked capability no third-party app can use. Flow's closest paths: a phone hotspot (instant shared LAN, identical feel), invitation links, or a self-hosted relay for internet-wide pairing.
  • Sidecar / extended display. Flow's extended display works over Wi-Fi to another computer — experimental, and Windows receivers may need a virtual-display driver.
  • Universal Control. Flow's keyboard/mouse link covers the same ground in both directions — gated by approval, heartbeat-supervised, Escape to release.

Genuinely Apple-only — what Flow can't do

  • Transfers with no network at all. AWDL/Bluetooth handoff is hardware Apple reserves; no third-party app gets it.
  • Clipboard sync while the app is fully off. An OS restriction for anything not built into the system — Flow replays your clipboard the moment a device comes back, which is the same end result once it's running.
  • Zero-friction iOS integration. AirDrop is built into iOS; Flow joins iPhones via Safari today, with a native app planned.
Any app claiming "real AirDrop on Windows" is overstating it — those radios are private. The honest promise is identical behavior on your network, and the closest possible equivalents beyond it.

Where Flow actually goes past AirDrop

  • It isn't Apple-only. Windows, Mac and Android in one space — that's the entire point.
  • Friend sessions. Invite someone outside your devices with an isolated space that excludes your personal clipboard.
  • Queue & resume. Sends wait for reconnecting devices and resume mid-file after drops.
  • Self-hostable relay for people who want internet-wide pairing on their own infrastructure.

The non-Apple devices deserve this too.

Same discovery, same approval, same effortlessness — across every OS you own.
